# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Denver Harbor / Port Houston locals — working people on a lunch break, families who've eaten here for generations, and folks across Houston who heard about the place and want to check it out. Most arrive on a phone, often in bright sun, wanting one thing fast: the menu, the hours, or the phone number to call an order in.

## Product Purpose

A simple single-page website for Dixie Maid, the decades-old burger stand at 6800 Lyons Ave in Denver Harbor, Houston. It exists so people can find the menu (fast, especially on mobile), see the hours, and call ahead. Success: a visitor gets from landing to the menu in one tap, and the site feels like the place — old-school, honest, unpretentious — while being a clean modern build.

## Positioning

The neighborhood burger stand that never changed and never needed to — same corner of Lyons Ave, burgers made when you order them, prices that still start under two bucks.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: See the menu (one tap from anywhere on the page).
- Secondary CTA: Call it in — (713) 674-3828.
- The line a visitor remembers: "Same corner. Same griddle."
- Belief ladder: (1) this is the real old-school spot, not a chain; (2) the food is made to order and worth the drive; (3) it's cheap, close, and open right now → look at the menu → call it in or drive over.
- Proof on hand: 4.6★ Google rating (~107 reviews), regulars quoted going "for over 30 years," the owner John known by name, real photos from the Google listing (the cheeseburger + hand-cut fries, the orange booths, the letterboard menu).

## Brand Personality

Down-to-earth, unhurried, proud without bragging. Voice in three words: plain-spoken, neighborly, nostalgic. Copy sounds like the person at the counter — short sentences, no corporate buzzwords, no "elevated" or "crafted" or "experience." Never call it a "brand." It's a burger stand.

## Anti-references

- Do not copy samburgerusa.com (the customer likes its simple structure — hero, fresh-daily points, menu, reviews, hours — but this must be its own thing).
- No corporate-chain language ("craveable," "signature flavors," "family of brands").
- No glossy dark "premium smash burger" aesthetic; no minimalist beige foodie-editorial look.
- Not a Wix/SpotHopper template feel.

## Design Principles

1. Menu first — the menu is the product; it lives one tap away at all times, especially on mobile.
2. Look like the place — the letterboard, the orange booths, the white tile, and the red sign are the design system; the real photos carry the page.
3. Plain talk — every line of copy passes the "would the guy at the counter say this?" test.
4. Modern build, old soul — fast, responsive, accessible; nostalgic in look, never in performance.
5. Nothing fancy for fancy's sake — if a section doesn't help someone eat here, cut it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG target stated; hold to WCAG 2.1 AA in practice — ≥4.5:1 body contrast, large tap targets (phone-first audience), reduced-motion alternatives, semantic landmarks, readable in bright outdoor light.
